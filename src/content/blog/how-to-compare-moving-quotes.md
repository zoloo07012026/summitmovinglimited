Three quotes for the same move can differ by hundreds of dollars, and the
cheapest is not always the cheapest. Usually the difference is not the hourly
rate. It is that each quote describes a slightly different job.

Here is what to line up before you compare the numbers.

## Is it an estimate or a price?

This is the first thing to establish, because it changes what the number means.

- A **non-binding estimate** is a prediction. The final bill is based on actual
  time or actual weight and can come in higher or lower.
- A **binding estimate** is a fixed price for the inventory and services
  listed. If the inventory is accurate, the price does not move.

Neither is wrong. But comparing a low non-binding estimate against a higher
binding one and picking the low number is not a comparison at all.

## Check that the quotes describe the same job

Ask each company to state these in writing, then put them side by side:

**Crew size and truck.** Two movers on a three-bedroom home will cost fewer
dollars per hour and more hours. Ask for the crew size behind the estimate.

**What the hours cover.** Some hourly quotes start when the crew arrives and
stop when they finish. Others include travel time to and from the depot. That
alone can be an hour or more.

**Packing materials.** Boxes, tape, shrink wrap, mattress bags and furniture
pads may be included, charged separately, or expected from you.

**Disassembly and reassembly.** Beds, tables and shelving usually need it.
Confirm whether it is in the price or added on the day.

**Access at both ends.** Stairs, long carries from the truck, elevator
bookings and shuttle trucks are the most common surprise charges. Describe
both addresses honestly when you ask for the quote - a quote written against a
description that turns out to be wrong is not a quote you can hold anyone to.

## Understand what "insured" actually covers

Every mover will tell you they are insured. That is about their liability, not
the replacement value of your belongings.

For interstate moves, federal rules give you a choice between two levels of
liability. The default, **released value**, is free and pays 60 cents per
pound per item. A 40-pound television is $24, whatever it cost. **Full value
protection** costs extra and covers repair or replacement.

Ask which one a quote assumes. Two quotes that differ by a few hundred dollars
may simply be quoting different coverage.

## Read the deposit terms

A modest deposit to hold a date is normal. Be careful with:

- A large deposit demanded in cash
- Any deposit before you have a written estimate
- Terms that are described verbally but not written anywhere

These are the most common signs of a company you do not want to have your
furniture.

## For interstate moves, check the registration

Any mover crossing state lines must be registered with the FMCSA and have a
USDOT number. You can look it up on the FMCSA's public site and see their
registration status and complaint history. It takes two minutes and it is the
single fastest way to rule a company out.

## A short checklist

Before you choose, make sure you have from each company:

1. A written estimate, marked binding or non-binding
2. Crew size and truck size
3. What the hours include, and whether travel is billed
4. Which materials are included
5. Any access charges for stairs, long carries or shuttles
6. The liability coverage the price assumes
7. Deposit amount and cancellation terms
8. For interstate, a USDOT number you have looked up

When all eight line up, the price comparison is finally meaningful.
